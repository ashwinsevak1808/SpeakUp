Introduction: Ten Minutes That Change a Life

Neha had a good degree, a good score and a good heart. But in her first campus interview, the panel asked, "Tell me about yourself," and she replied, "Sir, my name is Neha, I am from Nashik, I have done B.Com," and then went silent. She knew much more about herself. She simply had no plan for speaking about it. In her next interview she prepared five stories and three clear answers. This time she was selected.

An interview is not an exam. It is a conversation in which you help the panel imagine you in the job. In this chapter you will learn:

  • How to answer "Tell me about yourself" in one minute
  • The S.T.A.R. method for answering experience questions
  • How to handle strengths, weaknesses and tough questions
  • How to talk about salary and ask your own questions
  • What to do before, during and after the interview

Lesson 1Tell Me About Yourself

This is the most common opening question, and the one you can prepare fully. Use the Present, Past, Future formula and keep it near one minute.

  1. Present: who you are now. "I'm a final-year B.Com student at Pune University."
  2. Past: two or three relevant highlights. "I led our college fest's finance team and completed an Excel certification."
  3. Future: why this job. "I'm keen to start my career in accounts, and your company's training programme is a great fit."
Sample dialogue · The opening question

PanelPlease tell us about yourself.

NehaThank you. I'm Neha Patil, a final-year B.Com student from Nashik. In college I managed the budget for our annual fest, about two lakh rupees, and I completed an advanced Excel course. I enjoy working with numbers and solving small problems, so I'm very interested in this accounts trainee role.

PanelGood. What did you learn from managing the fest budget?

NehaI learnt to plan ahead and to keep honest records, because many people depended on those numbers.

Do not

Do not read your CV aloud and do not start from your birth. Your family, hobbies and village can come in one short line, only if they help the story.

Lesson 2The S.T.A.R. Method

When the panel asks, "Tell me about a time when you…", answer with a mini story in four steps:

LetterMeaningExample
SituationThe backgroundOur college fest was two days away and the sponsor backed out.
TaskYour responsibilityI had to arrange funds quickly.
ActionWhat you didI called local shops, offered banner space and collected fifty thousand rupees.
ResultWhat happenedWe held the fest on time and even saved ten percent.

Prepare five S.T.A.R. stories in advance: a challenge, a teamwork moment, a failure and what you learnt, a leadership moment, and a time you solved a problem. The same stories can answer many different questions.

Lesson 3Strengths, Weaknesses and Tough Questions

QuestionA good approach
What are your strengths?Name two, each with proof: "I'm a quick learner. I learnt Tally in two weeks."
What is your weakness?Choose a real, small one and show your fix: "I used to avoid public speaking, so I joined a speaking club."
Why should we hire you?Match your skills to the job: "You need accuracy and learning speed, and I bring both."
Where do you see yourself in five years?Show growth, not escape: "I want to grow into a senior role in finance with this company."
Why did you leave your last job?Stay positive: "I wanted more learning opportunities."
Do you have any questions?Always say yes. Ask one or two good questions.

If you do not know the answer

Do not panic and do not bluff. Say, "That's a good question. I don't know the exact answer, but here's how I would find out." Honesty plus a method is respected.

In Hinglish, the panic thought is: Mujhe iska jawab nahi aata. In English, turn it into: "I'm not sure, but I would approach it this way."

Lesson 4Salary, Notice Period and Your Questions

  • Expected salary: "Based on my research and skills, I'm expecting something in the range of X to Y. I'm open to discussing it." Research the market range first.
  • Notice period: "My notice period is thirty days, and it can be negotiable."
  • Your questions: "What does a typical day look like?" "What are the biggest challenges of this role?" "How will my performance be measured?"
Avoid asking early

Do not ask about leaves or holidays as your first question. Ask about the role, the team and growth.

Lesson 5Before, During and After

StageWhat to do
BeforeResearch the company, read the job description, prepare five stories, practise aloud, plan your travel, dress neatly, carry documents.
DuringGreet with a smile, firm handshake if offered, sit straight, listen fully, answer in 30 to 90 seconds, thank them at the end.
AfterSend a short thank-you email within 24 hours.

Tips and Tricks

Interview day
  • Reach 15 minutes early. Sit calmly and breathe slowly.
  • Pause for two seconds before answering. It sounds thoughtful, not slow.
  • Smile, look at the person who asked, and glance at the others as you speak.
  • Say "Could you please repeat the question?" if you did not hear it.
  • Record a mock interview on your phone and watch it once.

BWrite your own

Write a 60-second "Tell me about yourself" using Present, Past, Future. Say it aloud three times.

Show a sample

I'm Aarti Shinde, a final-year BCA student from Kolhapur. During college I built a small inventory app for a local shop and completed a Python course. I enjoy turning ideas into working tools, so I'm excited about this junior developer role.

CBuild a S.T.A.R. answer

Question: "Tell me about a time you worked in a team." Fill in S, T, A, R from your own life, in one sentence each.

Show a sample

S: Our team project was falling behind. T: I had to coordinate four members. A: I made a simple task chart and held a ten-minute call every day. R: We submitted on time and got the highest marks.

Try This Week · Mock Interview

Ask a friend or family member to interview you for ten minutes. Use these five questions: Tell me about yourself. What are your strengths? Describe a challenge you solved. Why should we hire you? Do you have any questions? Record it, watch it, and improve one thing.

Chapter Summary

A good interview answer is prepared, short and honest. Use Present-Past-Future for your introduction, S.T.A.R. for experience, give proof for strengths, be real about weaknesses and always ask a question at the end. Next, we move to the written word: emails and business writing.

Key takeaways
  • Prepare your "Tell me about yourself" as Present, Past, Future.
  • Use S.T.A.R. for "Tell me about a time…" questions.
  • Give evidence with every strength.
  • If you do not know, say how you would find out.
  • Always ask one or two questions and send a thank-you email.

Introduction: The Girl Who Knew the Answer

Priya sat in the third row of her college classroom in Pune. The professor asked, "Can anyone explain why the monsoon is important for India?" Priya knew the answer. She had read it twice. The sentence was ready in her head, first in her own language, then half in English: “Barish kheti ke liye bahut zaroori hai.” But her heart beat fast, her palms were wet, and she thought, What if I make a grammar mistake? What will everyone think?

She kept quiet. Someone else gave an average answer and got the praise.

If you have ever felt like Priya, this chapter is for you. Her problem was not English. Her problem was fear, and fear is a habit. Habits can change. In this chapter you will learn:

  • Why you already know more English than you believe
  • Why Indian learners feel shy, and why that is normal
  • How to stop translating and start thinking in English
  • How to treat mistakes as stepping stones
  • Five small habits that build confidence from day one

Lesson 1You Are Not Starting From Zero

Think of the English you use daily without effort:

busmobileWhatsAppexamticketdoctorhotelthank yousorryokayproblemtime

You understand film titles, road signs, app menus and reel captions. You have studied English for years in school. So why can't you speak it? Because school taught you to read and write for exams. Nobody trained you to speak. Speaking is a separate skill, like swimming. You cannot learn swimming by reading about swimming. You have to get into the water.

Remember

Knowing English and using English are two different things. You already know it. This book helps you use it.

Lesson 2Why We Feel Shy (And Why It Is Normal)

Almost every Indian learner carries one or more of these fears:

The fearThe thought in your headThe reality
Fear of mistakes"If I say it wrong, I'll look stupid."Listeners care about your message, not your grammar.
Log kya kahenge?"My friends will laugh."Most people are busy worrying about their own English.
Accent fear"My accent is too Indian."Clear speech matters, not a foreign accent. Indian English is a respected global variety.
Perfection trap"I'll speak only when I'm perfect."Perfection never arrives. Confidence arrives by speaking.

Notice that none of these fears are about English itself. They are about what others think. That is why you can chat with a close friend in broken English, but freeze in front of a stranger.

The Spotlight Effect

Psychologists call the feeling that everyone is watching you the spotlight effect. In truth people notice far less than you imagine. When you make a small mistake in class or a meeting, most people forget it within seconds. They are thinking about their own turn to speak.

Lesson 3Stop Translating, Start Thinking

Many learners build a sentence in their own language, translate it word by word, check the grammar, and only then speak. By then the conversation has moved on. It is slow and tiring, and it makes sentences sound odd.

Hinglish thoughtWord-by-word EnglishNatural English
Mujhe bhookh lagi hai.Hunger is coming to me.I'm hungry.
Main ghar par tha.I was on the house.I was at home.
Mujhe neend aa rahi hai.Sleep is coming to me.I'm sleepy.
Kitna time laga?How much time it took?How long did it take?

The Fix: Think in Small Chunks

Instead of translating whole sentences, learn ready-made chunks and use them like building blocks:

I'd like to…I'd like to order tea. Could you please…?Could you please repeat that? I think that…I think that the plan is good. What I mean is…What I mean is, we need time.

Chunks save your brain from building every sentence from scratch. You will meet many more of them in Chapters 5 and 7.

Lesson 4Mistakes Are Your Teachers

A baby does not learn to walk by sitting still. It falls hundreds of times, and nobody says, "You fell, you are a failure." Language works the same way. Every mistake you make and correct becomes a lesson you will not forget.

Sample dialogue · At the market

RaviBhaiya, how much is this shirt cost?

ShopkeeperFour hundred rupees.

RaviLittle bit expensive. Can you make it three fifty?

ShopkeeperOkay, three fifty. Final price.

Ravi made two small mistakes ("how much is this shirt cost" and "little bit"), yet he was understood and got his discount. That is the real goal: be understood first, be polished later.

Lesson 5Five Confidence Habits to Start Today

  1. Speak one sentence a day to someone. Even "Good morning, how are you?" to a neighbour counts.
  2. Talk to yourself. Describe what you are doing: "I'm making tea. I'm adding sugar." Nobody is judging.
  3. Collect your wins. Every night, write one thing you said in English that day.
  4. Slow down. Nervous people speak fast. Take a breath, speak at half speed, and you will sound calmer.
  5. Use the "Good Enough" rule. If the listener understood you, the sentence was good enough. Move on.

Tips and Tricks

Quick wins
  • Smile before you speak. It relaxes your voice and your face.
  • Start with safe people: a sibling, a friend, a roommate. Build up to strangers.
  • Use fillers wisely: "Let me think…", "Well…", "You know…" buy time without silence.
  • If you forget a word, describe it: "the thing you use to cut paper" (scissors). Native speakers do this too.
  • Ask for help: "How do you say this in English?" is a smart question, not a weak one.

Chapter Summary

You are not a beginner who knows nothing. You know a lot, and fear is simply standing in the way. Speaking is a skill that grows with use, mistakes are part of that growth, and being understood matters more than being perfect.

Key takeaways
  • You already know many English words. Start using them.
  • Fear of Log kya kahenge? is common and fixable.
  • Learn chunks, not word-by-word translation.
  • Be understood first, be perfect later.
  • Small daily speaking beats occasional long study.

Introduction: Two Skills, One Goal

Karan wanted to buy a second-hand laptop. The seller asked for forty thousand rupees. Karan did not shout or walk away. He said calmly, "I like the laptop. The battery is weak, so would thirty-two thousand work?" They settled at thirty-four. Later, when Karan presented his startup idea, he did not begin with numbers. He began with a story about his mother struggling to find a local tailor. People leaned in and listened.

Negotiation helps you reach agreement. Storytelling helps people care and remember. Both are about understanding people. In this chapter you will learn:

  • The four steps of a calm, fair negotiation
  • Phrases for bargaining, requests and polite refusals
  • How to say "no" without damaging a relationship
  • A simple story structure you can use anywhere
  • How to make your stories vivid and short

Lesson 1The Four Steps of Negotiation

  1. Prepare. Know what you want, your minimum acceptable result and the other side's likely needs.
  2. Listen. Ask questions first: "What matters most to you in this deal?"
  3. Propose. Make a clear offer with a reason: "Considering the battery, I can offer thirty-two thousand."
  4. Close. Summarise what you agreed and confirm: "So, thirty-four thousand with the charger included. Is that correct?"
The win-win idea

The best negotiation leaves both sides satisfied. Ask yourself: "What can I give that costs me little but matters to them?" For example, quick payment, a longer contract or a good review.

Lesson 3Saying "No" Politely

In Indian culture we often say "Haan ji" even when we mean no, because we fear hurting people. This leads to stress and broken promises. A polite, clear "no" actually builds trust.

Instead of…Try
Okay, okay, I will see (but you mean no)Thank you for asking, but I won't be able to do that.
I can't. Bye.I'd love to help, but I'm fully booked this week. Could we do it next week?
It's impossible.That's difficult for me right now. Here's what I can do instead.

The pattern is Appreciate, Decline, Offer an alternative. In Hinglish you might think Mujhse yeh nahi ho payega. Say it as: "I'm afraid I can't take this on right now, but I can help with…"

Lesson 4The Storytelling Structure

People forget facts but remember stories. Use this simple five-step shape for any story, from a class talk to an interview answer.

  1. Hook: open with an interesting moment. "The day my train stopped for six hours…"
  2. Character and setting: who and where, in one line.
  3. Problem: what went wrong or what was at stake.
  4. Turning point: what changed or what you did.
  5. Lesson: what it means for the audience.
A tiny example

"Last monsoon, my train stopped for six hours near Kalyan. I had an interview in two hours. I walked to the nearest road, asked a truck driver for a lift and reached on time, wet and nervous. I learnt that preparation includes a Plan B."

Lesson 5Make Your Stories Vivid

  • Use specifics: "a blue plastic chair in a crowded waiting room" beats "a place".
  • Use the senses: sounds, smells, weather.
  • Use dialogue: "He said, 'Beta, don't worry.'" brings people alive.
  • Use pauses: a pause before the key moment builds interest.
  • Keep it short: two minutes is plenty. Cut anything that does not serve the lesson.

Tips and Tricks

Negotiate and tell like a pro
  • Say your number first only if you have researched it. Otherwise ask for theirs.
  • Stay calm. A relaxed voice shows strength.
  • Silence is a tool. After you make an offer, wait.
  • Collect five personal stories: a failure, a win, a funny moment, a lesson from family, and a surprise.
  • Practise telling each story in 90 seconds.

Chapter Summary

Negotiation is prepared, calm and fair. Listen, propose with a reason and aim for a win-win. Saying no politely protects relationships. Stories give your words life: use Hook, Character, Problem, Turning point and Lesson. This closes Part 4. In Part 5 we build your growth plan, starting with a 30/60/90-day roadmap.

Key takeaways
  • Prepare, listen, propose, close.
  • Look for a win-win, not a win-lose.
  • Say no with Appreciate, Decline, Alternative.
  • Stories need a hook, a problem, a turning point and a lesson.
  • Be specific, vivid and short.

Introduction: When You Cannot See the Face

A phone call is harder than a face-to-face chat. You cannot see the other person's smile or gestures, so you depend only on words and voice. No wonder many people feel nervous when an unknown number calls. But phone English follows a small set of patterns, and once you know them, calls become routine. The same patterns help in social situations too: introductions, invitations, thanks, apologies and polite refusals.

In this chapter you will learn:

  • How to open, hold and close a phone call
  • How to spell names and numbers clearly
  • Customer-care and office call phrases
  • How to introduce people
  • How to say "no" politely, and to thank and apologise well

Lesson 1The Phone Call Structure

  1. Open. "Hello, this is Rohan speaking." (Not "I am Rohan speaking.")
  2. State your purpose. "I'm calling about the job opening you posted."
  3. Listen and respond. Use short signals: "I see." "Right." "Okay."
  4. Confirm. "So, I'll send the documents by Friday. Is that correct?"
  5. Close. "Thank you for your time. Have a great day. Bye."

Lesson 2Spelling and Numbers

Names and numbers are the easiest things to mishear on a phone. Spell them with a simple word for each letter.

A for AppleB for BoyD for DelhiM for MumbaiP for PuneS for SamT for TomV for Victor
  • Say numbers in small groups with a pause: "nine eight seven six five, four three two one zero".
  • For double digits, say double: "double five" for 55 (common in India and the UK).
  • Repeat the number back to confirm: "Let me repeat that…"

Handy phrases

As a customer: "I'd like to report a problem." "Could you tell me the status of my request?"   As an agent: "I understand your concern." "Please bear with me for a moment." "Is there anything else I can help you with?"

Lesson 5Saying "No" Politely, Thanking and Apologising

A polite "no" has three parts: a soft start, a short reason, and a kind alternative.

PartExample
Soft start"Thank you so much for inviting me, but…"
Short reason"…I already have a family function that day."
Alternative"Maybe we can meet next weekend?"
I'd love to, but I can't.I'm afraid I can't make it.Thanks for the offer, but I'll pass.Sorry, I'm already booked.

Thanking: "Thanks a lot." "I really appreciate your help."   Apologising: "I'm so sorry for the delay." "Sorry about that. It won't happen again." Say the reason briefly and move on. Long excuses sound less sincere.

In Hinglish: Aane ki koshish karunga, par pakka nahi bol sakta. → I'll try to come, but I can't promise.

Tips and Tricks

Phone confidence
  • Smile while you speak. It changes your voice, and the listener can hear it.
  • Keep a pen and paper ready. Write the key words as the person speaks.
  • Speak a little slower and clearer than you do face to face.
  • Prepare 3 key points before you make an important call.
  • If you did not understand, say so right away. A repeated question beats a wrong action.

Chapter Summary

Phone calls and social situations feel difficult only until you know the pattern. Open clearly, state your purpose, confirm, and close warmly. In social life, a polite "no", a quick "thank you" and a sincere "sorry" will take you a long way. Next, you will train your ears for the other half of every conversation: listening.

Key takeaways
  • Say "This is ___ speaking", not "I am ___ speaking".
  • Spell names with simple words, and read numbers in groups.
  • Confirm important details at the end of a call.
  • Introduce people with a short, friendly sentence.
  • A polite "no": soft start, short reason, kind alternative.

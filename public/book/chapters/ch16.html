Introduction: The Email That Got Ignored

Sagar sent his manager an email at 11 p.m. The subject said "Hello". The body ran to four long paragraphs with no clear request. Next morning there was no reply. His colleague Divya sent a three-line email with the subject "Leave request: 12 to 14 March", and got approval in ten minutes. The difference was not English skill. It was clarity.

Good business writing is short, polite and easy to act on. In this chapter you will learn:

  • The structure of a clear professional email
  • Subject lines, greetings and closings that work
  • Ready-to-use templates for common situations
  • How to write on WhatsApp, Teams and LinkedIn at work
  • How to proofread quickly

Lesson 1The Five-Part Email

  1. Subject line: specific and short. "Meeting request: Tuesday 3 p.m." not "Hi" or "Urgent!!!"
  2. Greeting: "Dear Mr Rao," or "Hi Priya,".
  3. Opening line: why you are writing. "I'm writing to request a day off on Friday."
  4. Details: short paragraphs or bullets with the facts and the action you need.
  5. Closing: "Thank you." "Kind regards," followed by your name.

Be polite, not dramatic

In Hinglish you may write Sir, kripya meri madad kijiye. In English, keep it simple and respectful: "Could you please help me with this?" Avoid "Kindly do the needful ASAP". It sounds old-fashioned and pushy.

Lesson 3Templates You Can Reuse

Template 1 · Leave request

SubjectLeave request: 12 to 14 March

EmailDear Mr Rao, I would like to request leave from 12 to 14 March for a family function. I will complete my pending tasks before leaving, and Priya will cover urgent work. Please let me know if you need any more information. Thank you. Kind regards, Divya

EmailDear Sir/Madam, I am a second-year B.Sc. student. Could you please tell me whether students from my year can apply for the summer internship, and what documents are required? Thank you for your time. Sincerely, Ritu Jadhav

Lesson 4WhatsApp, Teams and LinkedIn at Work

  • Chat apps: Keep messages short. Greet once, then get to the point. Avoid voice notes longer than a minute for formal matters.
  • Emojis: A thumbs-up or smile is fine with colleagues. Skip them with clients you do not know.
  • LinkedIn: Write a clear headline ("B.Com graduate | Excel and Tally | Seeking accounts roles"), a short summary and polite connection notes: "Hi Mr Joshi, I enjoyed your talk on finance careers. I'd be glad to connect."
  • Reply time: Aim to acknowledge messages within a few hours: "Received, I'll update you by tomorrow."

Lesson 5Proofread in 60 Seconds

  1. Read it aloud once. You will catch awkward parts.
  2. Check names, dates, numbers and attachments.
  3. Cut extra words. "Due to the fact that" becomes "because".
  4. Check the tone. Would you be comfortable receiving it?
  5. Add the recipient last, so you do not send by mistake.

Tips and Tricks

Write like a pro
  • One email, one main purpose. Put the request in the first two lines.
  • Use bullets for lists of three or more items.
  • Put dates and deadlines in the subject or first line.
  • Reply to emails within 24 hours, even if only to say "Received".
  • Keep a "templates" note on your phone for repeated emails.

Chapter Summary

Strong professional writing is clear, short and polite. Use the five-part email, a specific subject line, the right tone and tested templates. Remove old-fashioned phrases and proofread before you press Send. Next, we use words to influence others through negotiation and storytelling.

Key takeaways
  • A specific subject line gets faster replies.
  • Put the request in the first two lines.
  • Match tone to the reader: formal, semi-formal or casual.
  • Avoid "do the needful" and "revert back".
  • Read aloud and check names, dates and attachments before sending.

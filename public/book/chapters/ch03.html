Introduction: The Map Before the Journey

Imagine you are in Mumbai and want to reach Pune. Before you start, you must know two things: where you are now, and where you want to go. Learning English is the same. Many learners never ask "Where am I today?" and so they study random things for months without feeling any progress.

In this chapter you will check your English honestly but kindly. This is not a test. Nobody will judge your score. It is just a map. You will learn:

  • The four skills of English and how each one feels at different levels
  • A 12-question self-assessment with your result
  • What your level means and where to focus
  • How to set one clear, achievable goal
  • How to record your "Day 1" voice so you can see progress later

Lesson 1The Four Skills

English has four parts. Most people are strong in some and weak in others, and that is normal.

Speakingsaying your ideasListeningunderstanding othersReading & Vocabularyknowing wordsGrammar & Writingmaking correct sentences
Indian learners, a common pattern

Reading and grammar are usually stronger (thanks to school exams). Speaking and listening are usually weaker. That is exactly what this book trains.

Read each statement. Tap the number that is true for you today: 0 = Not yet, 1 = Sometimes, 2 = Usually, 3 = Easily.

Lesson 4Find Your Weakest Skill

Look at your answers again. The four questions in each group measure one skill. The group with the lowest total is your weakest skill. Do not feel bad. It is simply the place where practice will pay off the fastest.

If your weakest skill is…Do this first

VocabularyLearn 3 chunks a day with your own examples
GrammarFix the 5 mistakes you repeat most (Chapter 19)

Lesson 5Set One Clear Goal

"I want to improve my English" is too vague. A good goal is specific, small and has a date.

Vague goalClear goal
I want to speak fluent English.In 30 days, I will speak for 2 minutes about my day without stopping.
I want a good job.In 60 days, I will answer "Tell me about yourself" confidently in 1 minute.
I will improve my vocabulary.Every day for 30 days, I will learn 3 new phrases and use each in a sentence.
Sample dialogue · Talking about your goal

MentorSo, what do you want from English?

KaranI want to speak well in my campus interview.

MentorGood. By when?

KaranPlacements start in three months.

MentorThen let's aim to practise "Tell me about yourself" every day for 20 days.

Write your goal in Hinglish first if you like, then translate it into English: Mujhe 30 din mein do minute English bolni aani chahiye. → I want to be able to speak English for two minutes in 30 days.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

WrongRight
My English is very weak, I can't speak.My English is improving. I'm working on my speaking.
I know the grammar but I can't speak fluently.I know the grammar, but I find it hard to speak fluently.
I want to improve my English very much.I really want to improve my English.
My goal is that I will speak confident.My goal is to speak confidently.

Tips and Tricks

Assess like a coach
  • Be honest, not harsh. Your score is a starting line, not a verdict.
  • Retake the self-assessment every 30 days and compare.
  • Ask a friend to rate your speaking too. Others often hear more progress than you do.
  • Keep only one main goal at a time. Too many goals means no goals.

Practice Exercises

AMake the goal clear

Rewrite each vague goal as a clear one (with a number and a time):

  1. I want to speak better.
  2. I want to learn more words.
  3. I want to be confident in interviews.
Show sample answers
  1. In 30 days I will speak for 2 minutes about my day without stopping.
  2. Every day this month I will learn 3 new phrases and use them in sentences.
  3. In 45 days I will answer 10 common interview questions aloud without notes.
BCorrect the sentence
  1. My English is very weak, I can't speak at all.
  2. My goal is that I will speak confident.
  3. I am wanting to improve my pronunciation.
Show answers
  1. My English is improving, and I'm working on my speaking.
  2. My goal is to speak confidently.
  3. I want to improve my pronunciation.
CSay it in English
  1. Mujhe interview mein confidence ke saath bolna hai.
  2. Meri English dheere-dheere better ho rahi hai.
Show answers
  1. I want to speak confidently in an interview.
  2. My English is slowly getting better.
Try This Week · Record Your Day 1 Voice

Record a one-minute voice note answering: "Tell me about yourself and why you want to learn English." Do not edit. Do not redo. Save it as "Day 1". Then write your one clear goal on a sticky note and stick it where you see it daily. You will listen to this recording again in Chapter 18.

Chapter Summary

You cannot reach a destination without knowing your starting point. You now know your level, your weakest skill and your first goal. Part 1 is done: you have the mindset, the daily system and the map. Part 2 builds your foundations, starting with pronunciation.

Key takeaways
  • English has four skills, and most Indian learners are weakest in speaking and listening.
  • Your score is a map, not a judgement.
  • Focus first on your weakest skill.
  • One clear goal: specific, small, with a date.
  • Record Day 1 now so you can hear your progress later.

Introduction: Just the Grammar You Need

Grammar is not a punishment. It is the glue that holds your words together. And here is good news: to speak well in daily life you do not need all of English grammar. You need about seven small areas, used correctly and often. This chapter gives you exactly those, in the way you will actually use them: while talking.

In this chapter you will learn:

  • The five tenses you need for 90% of conversations
  • How to make questions without mistakes
  • Modal verbs: can, could, should, must
  • A, an and the, and the little words in, on, at
  • The mistakes that Indian learners repeat most, and quick fixes

Lesson 1The Five Tenses You Really Need

TenseUse it forExample
Present simplehabits, facts, routinesI drink tea every morning.
Present continuoushappening now or around nowI am studying for my exam.
Past simplefinished actions, with a timeI met him yesterday.
Present perfectexperience, or something that connects to nowI have visited Goa twice.
Future (will / going to)plans, promises, predictionsI am going to start a course next month.
Remember

Use past simple when you say when (yesterday, last year, in 2020). Use present perfect when you do not say when. "I saw the film yesterday." but "I have seen that film."

In Hinglish: Main use kal hi mila. → I met him yesterday.  |  Main Goa do baar ja chuka hoon. → I have been to Goa twice.

Lesson 2Verbs You Don't Use with -ing

Some verbs describe states, not actions. We usually do not use them in the continuous form.

WrongRight
I am knowing the answer.I know the answer.
I am wanting some water.I want some water.
She is liking this song.She likes this song.

Other common ones: need, believe, understand, love, prefer, remember.

Lesson 3Making Questions

In English questions, the helper verb (is, are, do, does, did, can) goes before the subject.

StatementQuestion
You are working.Are you working?
He likes cricket.Does he like cricket?
They went home.Did they go home?
You are doing what?What are you doing?

Notice: after does and did, the main verb goes back to its base form: "Does he like…?" not "Does he likes…?"

Sample dialogue · Meeting someone new

RaviWhere do you live?

SanaI live in Kothrud. What about you?

RaviI live in Hadapsar. How long have you lived here?

SanaFor about five years. Did you grow up in Pune?

RaviNo, I grew up in Nashik.

Lesson 4Can, Could, Should, Must

Modal verbs help you ask, advise and express need. After a modal, always use the base verb, with no "to" and no "-s".

ModalUseExample
canability, informal permissionI can swim.
couldpolite requestCould you help me, please?
shouldadviceYou should sleep early.
muststrong need or ruleYou must wear a helmet.
may / mightpossibilityIt might rain later.

Lesson 5A, An, The

  • a / an for something new or one of many. Use an before a vowel sound: an apple, an hour, a university (it starts with "yoo").
  • the for something specific that both people know: "The book you gave me is great."
  • No article for general plural or uncountable ideas: "I like music." "Dogs are loyal."

Lesson 7Subject and Verb Must Agree

With he, she, it (singular), the present-simple verb takes an -s. With I, you, we, they, it does not.

WrongRight
She don't like coffee.She doesn't like coffee.
He go to office by bus.He goes to the office by bus.
My friends is coming.My friends are coming.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

WrongRight
What you are doing?What are you doing?
I have seen him yesterday.I saw him yesterday.
She is more better than me.She is better than me.
I am having a car.I have a car.
I am living in Pune since 2020.I have lived in Pune since 2020.
She can to swim.She can swim.

Tips and Tricks

Learn grammar through speaking
  • Do not memorise rules. Memorise model sentences and change the words.
  • Fix one mistake at a time: choose your most frequent error and watch for it for a week.
  • If you are unsure, use a short, simple sentence. Short sentences are correct more often.
  • Record yourself, listen, and note any mistake you hear. Self-correction is the fastest teacher.
  • Do not stop mid-sentence to check grammar. Finish the idea and fix it next time.

Practice Exercises

AChoose the right tense
  1. I (go / went) to the market yesterday.
  2. She (works / is working) at a bank. She joined in 2022.
  3. I (have seen / saw) that film. It is very good.
  4. Look! It (rains / is raining).
Show answers
  1. went
  2. works
  3. have seen
  4. is raining
BMake the question

Change each statement into a question:

  1. He plays cricket on Sundays.
  2. You lived in Mumbai.
  3. She can drive.
Show answers
  1. Does he play cricket on Sundays?
  2. Did you live in Mumbai?
  3. Can she drive?
CCorrect the sentence
  1. What you want to eat?
  2. She don't like spicy food.
  3. I am knowing him very well.
  4. I have met her last week.
Show answers
  1. What do you want to eat?
  2. She doesn't like spicy food.
  3. I know him very well.
  4. I met her last week.
DSay it in English
  1. Tum kya kar rahe ho?
  2. Main 2020 se Pune mein reh raha hoon.
Show answers
  1. What are you doing?
  2. I have lived in Pune since 2020. (or: I have been living in Pune since 2020.)

Chapter Summary

You do not need all of grammar. Five tenses, correct questions, a few modals, articles, prepositions and agreement will carry most of your conversations. Learn them as model sentences, fix one mistake at a time and keep speaking. In the next chapter, you will learn how to turn your thoughts into sentences without translating word by word.

Key takeaways
  • Five tenses cover most everyday talk.
  • Do not use "-ing" with state verbs like know, want, like.
  • Questions: helper verb first. After do, does and did, the main verb stays in base form.
  • After a modal, use the base verb: can swim, should go.
  • Fix your top 3 mistakes, one week at a time.

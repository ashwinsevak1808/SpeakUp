Introduction: Stop Translating in Your Head

You think of a sentence in your own language, translate it word by word, check the grammar, and then speak. By then the conversation has moved on. This is the reason many learners with good vocabulary and grammar still feel slow. The fix is not more knowledge. It is a new habit: going straight from idea to English.

In this chapter you will learn:

  • Why word-by-word translation fails, and what to do instead
  • The Picture, Keywords, Sentence method
  • Sentence frames you can fill in on the spot
  • Connecting words that make you sound smooth
  • How to say "um" less, and think aloud in English

Lesson 1Why Translation Fails

Languages do not match word for word. Look at what happens when we translate directly.

Word-by-word (wrong)Natural English (right)
Mujhe bhookh lagi hai. → "To me hunger has been attached."I'm hungry.
Mera sir dard kar raha hai → "My head is paining."I have a headache.
Aapka naam kya hai? → "Your name what is?"What's your name?
Mujhe neend aa rahi hai → "Sleep is coming to me."I'm sleepy.
The lesson

Do not translate words. Carry the meaning across and let English choose its own words. That is why you learned chunks in Chapter 5.

Lesson 2Picture, Keywords, Sentence

A simple three-step habit that rewires your thinking.

  1. Picture it. Do not think in words. See the idea in your mind: a bus, a person waiting, rain.
  2. Pick 2 or 3 keywords. Choose only the main English words: bus, late, rain.
  3. Build the sentence. Put the keywords into a simple frame: "The bus was late because of the rain."

Practise with objects around you. Look at a cup and say cup, hot, table → "The hot cup is on the table." Within two weeks you will notice that English words appear on their own.

Lesson 3Sentence Frames: Fill in the Blanks

A frame is a ready-made structure. You only change the blanks. Learn these and you can speak about almost anything.

PurposeFrameExample
OpinionI think that ___ .I think that online classes are useful.
ReasonThe reason is that ___ .The reason is that they save travel time.
ExampleFor example, ___ .For example, I can attend from home.
PreferenceI would rather ___ than ___ .I would rather walk than take a rickshaw.
PlanI'm planning to ___ .I'm planning to join a gym.
Contrast___ , but ___ .It is cheap, but it is not very comfortable.

Lesson 4The Ladder: Short to Long

Start with a short sentence and add one piece at a time. Each step is easy, and the result is a rich sentence.

  1. I like tea.
  2. I like strong tea.
  3. I like strong tea in the morning.
  4. I like strong tea in the morning because it wakes me up.

Speak all four steps aloud. This trains your mouth to build longer sentences naturally.

TeacherDo you prefer studying alone or in a group?

AnilI prefer studying in a group because we can discuss doubts. However, sometimes it is noisy, so I also study alone at night.

TeacherThat is a balanced answer. Can you give an example?

AnilYes. For example, last week my friend explained a maths concept in five minutes.

Lesson 6Replace "Umm" with Thinking Phrases

Pauses are natural. What matters is how you fill them. Instead of a long "ummm", use a short thinking phrase. It sounds confident and gives you time.

Well,…Let me think…That's a good question.How can I put it?To be honest,…Actually,…

And when you are lost, take one calm breath, say "Let me think," and use Picture, Keywords, Sentence.

Common Mistakes and Corrections

Wrong (translated)Right
My head is paining.I have a headache.
I am having doubt in this question.I have a doubt about this question. / I'm not sure about this.

I will tell you one thing, listen.Let me tell you something.
Today I am feeling sleep.I'm feeling sleepy today.

Tips and Tricks

Think in English every day
  • Narrate small actions silently: "I'm making tea. I'm adding sugar."
  • Keep your sentences short and clear. Long translated sentences get tangled.
  • Learn the English name of 5 things around you every day, and describe them.
  • Dream big, speak small. A simple correct sentence beats a fancy broken one.
  • Allow imperfect English. Fluency grows from speaking, not from waiting to be perfect.

Try This Week · The Three-Object Game

Every day, look at any three objects around you. For each one, use Picture, Keywords, Sentence and say one English sentence aloud. On Day 4, join your three sentences with and, but and because. On Day 7, record a one-minute description of your room without any translation in your head.

Chapter Summary

Fluency is not translating faster. It is skipping translation. See the picture, pick the keywords, use a frame, and connect with simple linking words. Part 2 is complete: you now have clear sounds, useful chunks, essential grammar and a way to turn thoughts into sentences. In Part 3, you will use all of them in real situations: greetings, small talk, shopping and travel.

Key takeaways
  • Word-by-word translation gives unnatural English. Carry the meaning, not the words.
  • Use Picture, Keywords, Sentence to think directly in English.
  • Learn sentence frames and fill in the blanks.
  • Grow sentences with the ladder method; link ideas with because, but, so and however.
  • Replace "umm" with a short thinking phrase.

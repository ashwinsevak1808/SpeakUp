Introduction: Slides Support You, They Don't Replace You

We have all sat through a presentation where the speaker simply read out a slide full of tiny text. Nobody remembers it. A good presentation is different: it has one clear message, a simple structure, clean slides and a speaker who connects with the audience. The speaker is the star, and the slides are the helper.

In this chapter you will learn:

  • How to plan around your audience and one key message
  • A simple flow for any presentation
  • Slide rules that keep your design clean
  • Phrases to guide the audience from point to point
  • How to handle questions confidently

Lesson 1Plan: Audience, Purpose, Message

Before you open PowerPoint, answer three questions on paper:

  1. Who is my audience? What do they already know? What do they care about?
  2. What is my purpose? To inform, to persuade, or to ask for a decision?
  3. What is my one key message? If the audience remembers only one sentence, what should it be?

Lesson 2A Simple Flow

PartWhat to doPhrase you can use

PreviewTell them what you will cover."Today I'll cover three things: the problem, our solution and the next steps."
Main pointsCover each point with data or an example."Let's start with the first point."
SummaryRepeat the key message."To sum up, we need to…"
QuestionsInvite questions and close."I'd be happy to take any questions."

Lesson 3Slide Rules

Keep slides simple
  • One idea per slide. If you have two ideas, make two slides.
  • Few words. Use short keywords, not sentences. A good slide can be understood in 3 seconds.
  • Big text. Use a font size of 24 or more. If it cannot be read from the back, it is too small.
  • Pictures, charts and numbers beat paragraphs. Show one chart, not three.
  • Plain colours and good contrast. Dark text on a light background, or the opposite.
  • Never read the slide aloud. Say something that adds to it.

Lesson 4Guiding Phrases

These phrases are like road signs. They tell the audience where you are and where you are going.

Lesson 5Handling Questions

  1. Listen to the full question without interrupting.
  2. Pause and repeat or rephrase it so everyone hears it: "So you're asking about the timeline."
  3. Answer briefly and directly.
  4. Check: "Does that answer your question?"

Tips and Tricks

Present like a professional
  • Practise out loud with a timer. Cut your content if you run long. Never rush the end.
  • Know your first and last minute by heart. These are the moments people remember.
  • Stand beside the screen, not in front of it. Face the audience, not the slides.
  • Have a "backup slide" for questions you expect.
  • Check your laptop, cable and slides at least 15 minutes early.

Try This Week · The Three-Slide Talk

Prepare a three-slide presentation on any topic: a title slide with your key message, one slide with a single chart or picture, and a final summary slide. Present it aloud in three minutes using at least four guiding phrases. Ask a friend to give you one question, and practise the four-step answer.

Chapter Summary

A great presentation starts on paper: know your audience, purpose and key message. Keep slides clean, use guiding phrases as road signs, and handle questions with the four-step method. Remember: you are the presentation, and the slides are only your support. Next, you will learn how to hold your own in a group discussion.

Key takeaways
  • Plan: audience, purpose, one key message.
  • Flow: welcome, preview, main points, summary, questions.
  • One idea per slide, big text, few words.
  • Use guiding phrases to signal each step.
  • For questions: listen, repeat, answer, check.

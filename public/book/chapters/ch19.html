Introduction: Everybody Makes Them

Every language learner makes mistakes, and Indian English has its own familiar patterns. Many of them come from translating our mother tongue directly into English. The good news is that the same mistakes appear again and again, so if you fix the top thirty, your English improves dramatically. This chapter is your quick reference. Read it, mark the mistakes you make, and come back to it often.

In this chapter you will learn:

  • The most common mistakes in grammar, word choice and expressions
  • Why each mistake happens, with a simple fix
  • How to find and fix your own personal top three
  • A method for correcting mistakes without losing confidence

Lesson 1Tense and Verb Mistakes

WrongRightWhy
I am knowing the answer.I know the answer.Verbs like know, want, like and understand do not take -ing.
I am having a car.I have a car."Have" for possession is not used in the continuous form.
I am living here since 2019.I have been living here since 2019.Use present perfect with since / for.
She didn't went to school.She didn't go to school.After did / didn't, use the base verb.
He is knowing English well.He knows English well.Simple present for facts.
I have seen that movie yesterday.I saw that movie yesterday.With a finished time (yesterday) use simple past.

Lesson 2Articles, Prepositions and Plurals

WrongRightWhy
He is engineer.He is an engineer.Use a / an before a job.
I went to the market for buy vegetables.I went to the market to buy vegetables.Use "to + verb" for purpose.
I am good in English.I am good at English.Good at.
She is married with a doctor.She is married to a doctor.Married to.
I discussed about the plan.I discussed the plan."Discuss" needs no "about".
He gave me many advices.He gave me a lot of advice."Advice" is uncountable.
Our staffs are friendly.Our staff are friendly."Staff" has no plural -s.

Lesson 3Word Choice and Indian English Expressions

WrongRight
Do one thing, send me the file.Could you send me the file?
I will do the needful.I'll take care of it.
Please revert back.Please reply.
What is your good name?What's your name?
I have a doubt.I have a question.
Prepone the meeting.Move the meeting earlier.
I am going to home.I'm going home.
Out of station.Out of town.
I will eat your head.You are annoying me.
A note on Indian English

Some Indian English words, like "prepone", are widely understood in India. They are not wrong in India, but they may confuse an international listener. Choose the form that suits your audience.

Lesson 4Mother-Tongue Traps

Many mistakes happen because Hindi or Marathi sentence patterns sneak into English.

Your thoughtWord-by-wordNatural English
Mujhe pata nahi.To me knowledge is not.I don't know.
Mera dost aaya hai.My friend has come, he is coming.My friend has arrived.
Kitne baje?How many o'clock?What time?
Mujhe tumse milna hai.I have to meet with you.I'd like to meet you.
Aap kahan se ho?You are from where?Where are you from?

The fix is simple: learn chunks. Ask, "How would an English speaker say this idea?", not "What is the word for this word?"

Lesson 5Pronunciation and Spoken Habits

  • v / w: "very" and "wary" are different. Bite your lower lip for v.
  • Silent letters: "knife", "half", "walk", "castle" have silent letters.
  • Word stress: PHOtograph, phoTOGraphy, PHOtographer. Stress changes with the word.
  • Fillers: too many "like" and "you know" and "actually". Pause instead.
  • Speed: speaking too fast hides your words. Slow down by about ten percent.

How to Fix Your Personal Top Three

  1. Collect. For a week, write every mistake you notice, or ask a friend to note them.
  2. Rank. Pick the three that you repeat most often.
  3. Fix. Write the correct sentence five times and say it aloud ten times.
  4. Use. Make three new sentences with each correction in real situations.
  5. Check. After a week, see if the mistake has reduced.
Self-correction tip

When you notice a mistake, repeat the sentence correctly right away: "I am knowing… I mean, I know." Do not apologise or feel bad. Self-correction is a sign of progress.

Tips and Tricks

Learn from mistakes
  • Keep a "mistake notebook" with wrong and right sentences side by side.
  • Revise it every Sunday.
  • Read your corrections aloud, not silently.
  • Ask friends to correct you gently at the end, not in the middle.
  • Focus on mistakes that change the meaning first.

Chapter Summary

Most mistakes come from a few patterns: wrong tenses, missing articles, wrong prepositions and direct translation. Spot your own top three, correct them in real sentences and revise them every week. Mistakes are not failures, they are the map to your next improvement. In the final chapter, we collect the best resources and habits to continue your journey.

Key takeaways
  • Stative verbs (know, want, like) do not take -ing.
  • Learn the common prepositions: good at, married to, discuss (no "about").
  • Avoid direct translation; learn chunks instead.
  • Fix three mistakes at a time, not thirty.
  • Self-correction means you are improving.

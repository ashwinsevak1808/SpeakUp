Introduction: Words That Work Together

Think about how you speak your own language. You do not pick single words from a dictionary. You say whole phrases that fit together: chai peena, photo khinchna. English works the same way. Fluent speakers do not build every sentence word by word. They use ready-made chunks, and so can you.

In this chapter you will learn:

  • Why chunks beat word lists
  • The most useful kinds of chunks: collocations and everyday phrases
  • A simple system to learn 3 chunks a day
  • How to remember them with spaced revision
  • What to do when you forget a word in the middle of speaking

Lesson 1Chunks, Not Lists

A list of single words, like make, do, take, heavy, does not tell you how to use them. A chunk gives you the word and its natural partner.

Single wordUseful chunk
decisionmake a decision
phototake a photo
homeworkdo my homework
rainheavy rain
advicegive someone advice
Why it works

When you learn "make a decision" as one piece, you will never say "do a decision". Your mouth already knows the pair.

Lesson 2Collocations: Words That Belong Together

A collocation is a pair of words that native speakers naturally use together. English chooses its partners, and there is often no logic. Learn them as pairs.

In HinglishSay in English
Photo khinchnatake a photo
Faisla lenamake a decision
Tez baarishheavy rain
Time par pahunchnaarrive on time
Galti karnamake a mistake

Notice that the English verb is different from what you may expect: you make a mistake and take a photo. This is exactly why chunks help.

Lesson 4Learn 3 Chunks a Day

You do not need 100 new words a week. You need a small habit you will really keep. Here is a system that takes only 10 minutes a day.

  1. Collect (3 min). From what you heard or read today, pick 3 chunks that you would really use. Write each in a notebook or phone note.
  2. Make it yours (4 min). Write one true sentence about your own life for each chunk. "I'll get back to you tomorrow" is easier to remember if it is about your real work.
  3. Say it aloud (3 min). Read all three sentences aloud twice. Mouth memory is as important as eye memory.

Lesson 5Remember with Spaced Revision

We forget fast if we never revisit something. Revisit each chunk at growing gaps and it moves into long-term memory.

WhenWhat to do
Day 1Learn the chunk and write your sentence
Day 2Cover the English, look at your Hinglish sentence, say it aloud
Day 4Use it in a voice note or a chat
Day 8Quick revision from your notebook
Day 15 and Day 30Final reviews. If you remember it, it is yours.
Make a simple box

Put your chunks on small cards. When you remember one easily, move it to the "later" pile. When you forget it, keep it in the "again tomorrow" pile. Five minutes with the cards is enough.

Lesson 6When You Forget a Word

Everyone forgets words, even native speakers. Do not freeze. Use one of these rescue phrases and keep going.

Sample dialogue · Forgetting a word

CustomerI want a thing for... for opening bottles. What do you call it?

ShopkeeperA bottle opener?

CustomerYes, that's the word. Thank you! I forgot it for a moment.

ShopkeeperNo problem. Anything else?

  • Describe it: "It's a thing you use for…"
  • Use a simpler word: "big" instead of "enormous"
  • Ask for help: "What do you call it in English?"

Common Mistakes and Corrections

WrongRight
Let's discuss about the plan.Let's discuss the plan.
Please return back the book.Please return the book.
He is my cousin brother.He is my cousin.
I will revert back to you.I'll get back to you. / I'll reply to you.
She is out of station.She is out of town.
Do a decision soon.Make a decision soon.

Small note: A few Indian English words, like prepone, are used in India but are not understood in other countries. For an international audience, say move it earlier.

Tips and Tricks

Smart vocabulary habits
  • Prefer common words you can use today over rare, impressive words.
  • Learn the word with a partner word: not "heavy", but "heavy traffic".
  • Keep a phone note called "Chunks" and add to it during the day.
  • Read or listen to topics you love. You will remember cricket words faster than political ones.
  • Review before bed. The brain sorts memories during sleep.

For 7 days, learn 3 chunks a day using the Lesson 4 system. At the end of the week, you will have 21 chunks, each with your own sentence. On Sunday, record a 2-minute voice note and use at least 10 of them. Keep the recording for Chapter 18.

Chapter Summary

Vocabulary is not a race to know thousands of words. It is the habit of collecting useful chunks, making them personal and revisiting them. Three chunks a day, repeated at the right gaps, will give you more speaking power than any word list. In the next chapter, you will learn just enough grammar to hold those chunks together.

Key takeaways
  • Learn chunks and collocations, not isolated words.
  • Choose common, useful phrases first.
  • Make each chunk personal with a true sentence of your own.
  • Revise at Day 2, 4, 8, 15 and 30.
  • When you forget a word, describe it, simplify it or ask for help, and keep going.

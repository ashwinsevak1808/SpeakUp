Introduction: The Journey Continues

Congratulations. If you have come this far, you are no longer the person who sat quietly in the third row. You know how to practise, how to speak with structure and how to handle fear. But a book can only start the journey. Real fluency grows over years through habits, good company and good resources. This final chapter gives you all three.

In this chapter you will learn:

  • Free and low-cost resources for listening, reading, speaking and writing
  • How to build an English environment around you
  • Ten habits of confident speakers
  • How to keep growing after the 90 days
  • How to measure progress for the next year

Lesson 1Build an English Environment

You do not need to go abroad to live in English. Turn your phone, your playlist and your friends into your classroom.

Lesson 2Resources That Work

Choose a few and stay with them, rather than jumping between many. Free resources change over time, so search for the latest versions by name.

SkillResources to explore
ListeningBBC Learning English, TED Talks with subtitles, podcasts like "6 Minute English", English news channels
SpeakingLanguage exchange apps, speaking clubs, Toastmasters, college debate groups, voice-note practice with a partner
VocabularyA pocket notebook, flashcard apps, Oxford or Cambridge online dictionaries (learn collocations and pronunciation)
ReadingNewspapers like The Hindu or Times of India, simple short stories, graded readers, Indian authors such as R.K. Narayan or Ruskin Bond
WritingA daily diary, LinkedIn posts, short emails, writing communities
GrammarEnglish Grammar in Use (book), free grammar sites and short YouTube lessons on one topic at a time
Choose with care

Prefer resources that make you speak or write, not only watch. If you spend an hour watching lessons, spend at least ten minutes speaking them aloud.

Lesson 3Ten Habits of Confident Speakers

  1. Speak daily. Even five minutes counts.
  2. Read aloud. Newspaper headlines or short paragraphs.
  3. Learn chunks. Not single words.
  4. Record yourself. Listen, then improve one thing.
  5. Welcome feedback. Ask trusted people for kind corrections.
  6. Collect new words in context. Always with an example sentence.
  7. Think in English. Narrate small things in your head.
  8. Prepare for key moments. Interviews, presentations, important calls.
  9. Stay curious. Learn from every conversation.
  10. Be patient. Fluency is a long walk, not a sprint.

Lesson 4Find Your Speaking Community

Speaking improves fastest with other people. Look for them:

  • College: debate club, English literary society, placement-prep groups.
  • Work: volunteer to give a short update or present in team meetings.
  • Local: Toastmasters clubs or community speaking groups.
  • Online: language exchange partners, English-speaking Telegram or WhatsApp groups with clear rules.
  • Home: an "English hour" with siblings or friends.
Sample dialogue · Starting a speaking group

RahulI'm thinking of starting an English speaking group in our hostel. Would you like to join?

PoojaThat sounds great! How will it work?

RahulWe'll meet twice a week for thirty minutes. Each person speaks for two minutes on a topic, and we give one compliment and one suggestion.

PoojaCount me in. I'll invite two friends.

Lesson 5Beyond 90 Days

TimeGoal
3 to 6 monthsHold a ten-minute conversation on familiar topics without long pauses.
6 to 12 monthsGive a short prepared talk, attend an interview, write professional emails confidently.
1 to 2 yearsSpeak naturally in most situations, understand different accents and handle unplanned discussions.

Every three months, repeat the self-assessment from Chapter 3, record a one-minute voice note and compare it with your Day 1 recording. Set one new, specific goal each time.

In Hinglish: Dheere dheere, roz thoda thoda, bas rukna nahi hai. In English: "Slowly, a little every day, and never stop."

Common Mistakes and Corrections

WrongRight
I will start practising after my exams only.I'll start practising today, even for five minutes.
I have joined many apps but I don't use.I've joined many apps, but I don't use them.
I am waiting for a perfect time to speak.I'm going to speak now, even if it isn't perfect.
I am learning English since three years.I've been learning English for three years.

Tips and Tricks

Keep the flame alive
  • Keep one main resource for each skill and ignore the rest.
  • Teach someone else. Teaching is the best revision.
  • Celebrate every milestone with a small treat.
  • When you feel stuck, go back to Chapter 1 and read the courage challenge again.
  • Keep your Day 1 recording forever. It will make you proud.

Try This Week · Your One-Year Letter

Write a short letter to yourself one year from now: where you are, what you can do in English, and what you are proud of. Read it aloud, record it and keep it safe. Open it on the same date next year.

Chapter Summary and Closing Words

You now have the mindset, the foundation, the everyday skills, the professional skills and a growth plan. What remains is the simplest and hardest thing: to keep going. Surround yourself with English, practise daily, welcome mistakes, find good company and stay patient. One day soon, you will answer a question in class, give a presentation, or speak with a stranger, and you will realise that you did it without fear.

Speak up. The world is listening, and your voice matters.

Key takeaways
  • Build an English environment around you.
  • Choose a few good resources and use them consistently.
  • Practise the ten habits of confident speakers.
  • Join or start a speaking community.
  • Reassess every three months and set a new goal.

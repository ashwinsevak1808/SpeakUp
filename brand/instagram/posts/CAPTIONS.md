### post-02: Stop translating in your head
Do you think in Hindi (or Marathi, Tamil, Bengali...) and then translate into English? That is why speaking feels slow.

Try this for one week:
1. Think in short English sentences.
2. Learn phrases, not single words.
3. Speak out loud for 5 minutes every day.

### post-03: Don't say this. Say this.
"I am having a car" is one of the most common English mistakes.

We use "have" for owning something. Owning is a state, not an action, so we don't add -ing.
Correct: I have a car. I have two brothers. I have a headache.

Follow us for one fix every day.

### post-04: A or An?
It is the sound that matters, not the letter.
an hour (the h is silent)
a university (sounds like "you")
an MBA (sounds like "em")
a one-day course (sounds like "won")

Quick test: is it "a" or "an" European trip? Answer in the comments.

### post-05: Since or For?
FOR + a length of time: for 3 years, for two hours.
SINCE + a starting point: since 2020, since Monday.

Example: I have lived in Pune for 3 years, since 2023.

### post-08: 5 interview phrases

2. "One example that comes to mind is..."
3. "That's a great question. Let me think."
4. "What I learned from that was..."
5. "I'm confident I can add value by..."
